# Python-Creators-monetisation-potential
	A python project analysing how certain factors (AI use, age and gender) affect a creator's monetisation potential.

# What's in this repository?
	Digital poster of the insights attained
	An .ipynb file of the code used to generate graphs in poster (can opened in Jupyter notebook)
	Dataset files (.csv)

# Objective
We analysed the factors that drive monetisation potential on creatorhub - a fictional platform made for this project's intent purpose. THe factors were namely the level of AI use, as well as the age and gender of our user demographics.

# Summary
From the data, we concluded that videos with no AI usage have the highest engagement and monetisation potential. Demographics wise, older creators and male creators are the groups that tend to have a higher-value sold and higher monetisation potential.

# How does AI use affect video performance metrics and their monetisation potential?
For video clicks, we found that between videos with no AI usage and videos that were AI-assisted, the former had a higher number of clicks. One possible reason could be because users tend to click on videos with an interesting or eye-catching thumbnail and title. And this is where the differences between human skills and AI become noticeable.
While the app’s AI is not able to consistently produce good outputs, skilled users able to wield editing softwares can consistently produce eye catching visuals with also their own branding and identity. Compared to well-designed and well-titled videos as such, AI assisted videos may thus still pale in comparison and fail to effectively hook readers, thus clicks are less. 
But, unlike the trends for the other video performance metrics, Clicks increased from AI-assisted to AI-generated. In fact, clicks on AI-generated videos was the highest amongst all three levels of AI usage at an average of around 22,000. 
This could be because firstly, the AI feature on the platform makes generating AI content fuss-free, so more users churn out many AI-generated videos daily, flooding the users’ feed. With a higher proportion of AI-generated videos filling the feed, a higher proportion of clicks would go to these videos. This inundation of AI-generated videos has also been observed in many other social media platforms.
Secondly, this observation could also be because AI videos are all the craze right now, and users actually also click on AI videos because they do want to watch it. On various social media platforms, AI slop gets millions of views despite being low quality and of no substance.  For instance, a 16-second AI-generated clip on YouTube of a dog had garnered more than 230 million views within the first three months it was posted. Contrary to what users may have thought, effort may not translate to interest in video. In this case, low effort AI generated videos see more views and clicks than videos with no AI use or AI-assisted videos with more creator input and ideation.

# While AI-generated videos do get the most clicks, they also have the lowest completion rates and click-through rates
It boils down to AI videos lacking personal touch and quickly becoming repetitive. With nothing to differentiate them from other AI-generated videos, the content created may seem boring. Thus while users do click on these videos out of curiosity, they quickly lose interest and scroll away within seconds.
